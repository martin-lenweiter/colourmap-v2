### The three alliances (2025-2026 reshuffle)

The 2M (Maersk/MSC) era ended in early 2025. The board now has three alliances on Asia-Europe and Trans-Pacific lanes:

- **Gemini** — Maersk + Hapag-Lloyd. Hub-and-spoke discipline, schedule reliability as the headline pitch.
- **Premier** — ONE + HMM + Yang Ming. Successor to THE Alliance with MSC out.
- **Ocean Alliance** — CMA CGM + COSCO + Evergreen. Renewed through ~2032. Largest aggregate capacity of the three.

**MSC** operates outside the alliances on most lanes, leveraging its scale advantage directly.

### Orderbook posture

Industry orderbook-to-fleet is at the highest level since 2010 — roughly **33.5%** (Clarksons/Shipping Intelligence Hub). [confirmed 3-0]

- **CMA CGM** sits at **45.5%** — significantly above industry — signalling continued capex-led expansion under the Saadé family's growth doctrine.
- **COSCO** at 38.1%.
- The overall newbuild wave will hit water in 2026-2028 against weakening demand growth — **structural overcapacity is the dominant 2026-2028 narrative** unless Red Sea/Hormuz reroutings absorb the slack.

### Tanker majors (note)

Verification is light here; treat as starting handles, not anchors:

- **Frontline** (NYSE: FRO, John Fredriksen) — VLCC and Suezmax fleet, top-3 listed crude tanker operator.
- **Euronav** — Belgian-listed, Saverys-family control after 2024 governance shakeup, VLCC-heavy.
- **COSCO Shipping Energy Transportation** — Chinese state crude tanker arm.
- **Bahri** (National Shipping Company of Saudi Arabia) — Riyadh-listed, key Saudi crude carrier.

The 2026 Hormuz repricing is the dominant single event for this segment — see Section 3.

### Dry bulk (note)

Less consolidated than container. Key listed operators: **Star Bulk Carriers** (Petros Pappas), **Pacific Basin** (HK), **Eagle Bulk** (post-Star Bulk merger), **Diana Shipping**, plus the Geneva commodity-trader-owned fleets (Cargill, Bunge, Louis Dreyfus chartering desks).

## 3 · Iran / Hormuz crisis — 2025-2026 timeline

### Anchor events

- **June 2025 — the "12-day war".** Direct Iran-Israel military confrontation. Belfer Center, Clingendael, Washington Institute, and Long War Journal all document that Iran's "Axis of Resistance" — Hezbollah, Iraqi PMFs (Kataib Hezbollah, Harakat al-Nujaba), and largely the Houthis — **took no significant military action to defend Iran**. Kataib Hezbollah publicly declined to participate. Hezbollah was inactive. Houthi action limited to a symbolic 15 June Jaffa missile, characterised as serving "political and symbolic function" rather than meaningful military impact. [confirmed 2-1, with explicit caveat on Houthi *Red Sea* activity which is separate.]

- **28 February 2026 — Operation Epic Fury.** US-Israeli combined strikes — approximately 900 strikes over ~12 hours — killed Supreme Leader **Ali Khamenei**. Independently confirmed by Belfer Center (Harvard), Howden Re (specialty reinsurance broker), Al Jazeera, Axios, NPR, CNN, Britannica, Soufan Center, and a US House Republican Policy Committee memo. [confirmed 3-0]

- **4 March 2026 — Iran formally declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed".** [Wikipedia, Al Jazeera, Tufts Now — confirmed.]

- **19 March 2026 — Brent crude peaked at $119/bbl** (+46% from the ~$71 pre-war level). US gasoline briefly hit ~$3.94/gallon by 22 March. [CNBC, IEA — confirmed 2-1; the split is on rounding and exact dates, not direction.]

- **March 2026 — IEA released 400 M barrels**, the largest emergency release in IEA history. Volume covered roughly **20 days of typical Hormuz throughput**. [confirmed]

- **Late March 2026 — partial de-escalation signals**, war-risk premiums began easing. Structural "new normal" baseline is an **open question** — likely well above the 0.10-0.25% pre-war floor but below the 2-3% March 2026 peak.

### What flows through Hormuz vs Bab el-Mandeb

**Critical product framing.** The Strait of Hormuz is overwhelmingly a **tanker** chokepoint. The Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb is the **container** chokepoint.

| Strait | Primary traffic | Direct impact on container carriers (incl. CMA CGM) |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| **Hormuz** | ~20% of global oil (~20.9 M b/d H1 2025 per EIA), substantial LNG | Mostly *indirect*: bunker prices, customer-side oil shocks, war-risk reinsurance pass-through. Direct container transit limited. |
| **Bab el-Mandeb / Red Sea / Suez** | Container Asia-Europe artery | *Direct*: container ships rerouted via Cape of Good Hope when Houthis attack, adding 10-14 days, ~25% capacity absorption, and the resulting 2024-25 freight rate spike. |

Section 7 of the Geopolitics platform's Hormuz Crisis category should open with this framing — without it, readers confuse the two crises and misjudge CMA CGM's exposure.

### Insurance and pricing

**War-risk premiums** for Hormuz transit repriced approximately **12x** in March 2026:

- Pre-war: 0.10-0.25% of hull value.
- March 2026 peak: 2-3% of hull value, with the 3% ceiling specifically on US/UK/Israeli-linked vessels.
- Volatile within the month — Howden Re primary report shows peak ~2.5% early March, easing to ~1% late March.

[Howden Re primary, S&P Global, Caixin, Insurance Journal — confirmed 3-0]

This is the foundational pricing input for any CMA CGM-facing risk product.

### Vessel attacks (contested)

A claim of "at least 28+ vessels attacked between 28 Feb and 23 Mar 2026, single-day peak 11-12 March (16 vessels), named hulls including Nova, Stena Imperative, MKD VYOM, Hercules Star" was **refuted 1-2** by adversarial verifiers. The Howden Re primary source supported it, but corroborators did not converge on the specific vessel list. **Mark as unverified in platform content.**

### Regulation

- **EU ETS Maritime** — full compliance year 2026 (100% of CO₂ emissions on intra-EU voyages, 50% on extra-EU voyages calling at EU ports). [searoutes.com, cse-net.org — confirmed directional.]
- **FuelEU Maritime** — 2025 baseline GHG intensity target with stepped-down reductions through 2050.
- **IMO net-zero framework** — adopted 2025, kicks in operationally 2027-2030. Carriers ordering LNG and methanol dual-fuel are pricing in these regimes.

### Newbuild vs demand

Global orderbook at **15-year high** (Riviera Maritime), with 2025 activity easing. Combined with Red Sea reopening, the structural 2026-2028 story is **container overcapacity unless geopolitical disruption absorbs the slack**. This is the lever every alliance is watching.

## 5 · Geopolitical alignment map

### Core axis

- **Pro-Western:** United States, Israel, United Kingdom, European Union (with internal France-Germany nuance — France more independent stance).
- **Anti-Western core:** Iran with **Russia** (formalised) and **China** (asymmetric).
- **Iranian proxies (structurally degraded as of mid-2025/early 2026):** Hezbollah, Iraqi PMFs (Kataib Hezbollah, Harakat al-Nujaba), Houthis. *Did not act* to defend Iran in the 12-day war.

### Russia-Iran — formalised

- **December 2023 — Joint Declaration** on counteracting unilateral coercive measures (sanctions cooperation). [CSIS, Iran International — confirmed.]
- **January 2025 — 20-year Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Treaty.** Defense, cyber, energy, security. [TRENDS Research, INSS, Soufan Center — confirmed 3-0.]
- **Shared framing:** US and allies as principal security threat. CSIS verbatim.
- Caveat: the December 2023 instrument is technically a *joint declaration*, not a *treaty*; the formal treaty came January 2025.

### INSTC — the sanctions-bypass corridor

The **International North-South Transport Corridor** is a 7,200 km road-rail-naval route initiated in 2000 by Russia, Iran, India. Russia has accelerated commitment post-2022 Ukraine invasion as **explicit sanctions-bypass infrastructure**:

- Russia committing **€1.3 bn+** to the €1.6 bn **Rasht-Astara railway segment** (target completion Q3 2027).
- Iran-Russia signed a "transit roadmap for 2025" (18 February 2025).
- Putin/Pezeshkian reaffirmed Rasht-Astara commitment publicly.

CSIS verbatim: "a clear Russian and Iranian commitment to creating a trade route that could bypass Western sanctions." [confirmed 3-0]

### China — asymmetric

The Russia-China-Iran alignment is **strategic but not urgent** for Beijing — verbatim from CSIS. Beijing maintains a longer time horizon, declared neutrality through the March 2026 crisis, and pursues diversification.

Real exposure points:

- Iranian oil imports: shortfall of **1-1.4 mb/d** during the crisis (Diplomat/Al Jazeera reporting).
- China's Gulf crude imports dropped ~25% in March 2026.
- Belt and Road maritime stake (Gwadar in Pakistan, Djibouti, Piraeus, Haifa) creates significant chokepoint exposure.
- COSCO Shipping transits — verified 2026 response posture is an **open question** for the workflow.

China treats Hormuz as a logistics/price problem rather than existential.

### Refuted claims (do not publish without re-sourcing)

- "By early 2026 the Houthis abandoned regional 'axis of resistance' projection and refocused on domestic survival after US Operation Rough Rider 2025 (~1,100 airstrikes)." **Refuted 0-3.** The Belfer source asserted this; verifiers did not corroborate. Treat as contested.
- "Russia and China both reduced their support to Iran in 2025-2026 citing strategic overstretch / sanctions-risk aversion." **Refuted 1-2.** The opposite direction is better supported.
- Specific 28+ vessel attack tally in Hormuz March 2026 with named vessels — **refuted 1-2** (see Section 3 above).

### Gulf states — opportunity matrix (preliminary)

These positions were *framed but not separately verified in this pass*; treat as analytical starting points, not facts:

- **Saudi Arabia** — formal neutrality through the crisis, used East-West pipeline (Habshan-Fujairah and the cross-country pipeline) to maintain crude exports, quietly benefits from Iranian export disruption on price.
- **UAE** — Fujairah-bypass infrastructure becomes more valuable; Dubai trading houses gain. Continues Abraham Accords diplomatic posture with Israel.
- **Qatar** — LNG exporter, exposed to Hormuz, hedges via long-term contracts and mediating role.
- **Turkey** — opportunistic, leveraging the Bosphorus and pipelines, plays both sides.
- **Egypt** — Suez revenue dependency makes Red Sea normalisation an Egyptian national interest first.
