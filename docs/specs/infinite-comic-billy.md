# Billy Pineapple Infinite Comic

## Purpose

The infinite comic is a long-term **Entertainment** world inside Colourmap: relaxation, reflection,
play, and mythic wandering. It is not an Education program and should not sit in the thinker/comic
curriculum. It is a living myth engine: a comic, map, game, and psychological mirror where the user
can wander through symbolic worlds when they need to relax, recover, or understand themselves
indirectly.

Core promise:

```text
Explore worlds to understand yourself.
```

The comic should feel funny, beautiful, reflective, and alive. It should never become a productivity
tool, a therapy replacement, or a rigid personality quiz. It is a pocket labyrinth for imagination,
rest, and self-recognition.

## Core Character

The main character is **Billy the Pineapple**.

Billy works because he can be absurd and serious at the same time. A purely first-person point of
view would be immersive but less memorable. A ghost or soul traveler would be symbolic but less
funny. Billy gives the universe a comic body, expressive reactions, and emotional distance: the user
can laugh at Billy, then suddenly recognize themselves.

Billy is:

- curious
- sensitive
- stubborn
- easily convinced by strange advice
- looking for stability, happiness, a sofa, and The Juice
- not a chosen hero, just a pineapple trying to live

Recurring question:

```text
Is Billy a hero chasing destiny, or a simple pineapple on a quest for The Juice?
```

Billy's design should stay simple and iconic:

- round pineapple body
- simple arms and legs
- expressive eyes
- small shoes
- easy facial expression changes
- possible visual transformations based on choices
- warm Colourmap paper-comic universe

## Billy Design Reference

The working Billy design reference is a character-sheet style image on warm paper:

- large hand-drawn title: `Billy the Pineapple`
- round textured pineapple body with leafy crown
- thin flexible arms and legs
- expressive oversized eyes
- simple smile and readable facial expressions
- socks and small black sneakers
- optional hoodie, cap, backpack, notebook, jar of stars, and other props
- multiple body explorations: round pineapple, taller pineapple, square sponge-like variant, rough
  textured variant
- eye variations: innocent, tired, starry-eyed, suspicious, huge excited eyes
- emotion variations: happy, confused, angry, sad, shocked, delighted, unimpressed, inspired
- silhouette tests that keep Billy recognizable even as the body changes

This reference is the baseline for Billy:

```text
simple enough to animate
funny enough to carry absurd scenes
expressive enough for serious moments
iconic enough for merch
```

Billy should not become overdesigned. His power is that a small change in eyes, shoes, crown, or
posture can carry the whole emotional beat.

Design rules:

- keep the pineapple texture visible but not noisy
- keep limbs thin and flexible
- keep face readable at phone size
- preserve a strong silhouette
- use accessories to mark chapters, not to clutter the base design
- let transformed Billy versions still read as Billy
- prefer warm hand-drawn paper texture over shiny digital mascot polish

Merch implication:

Billy should work as:

- a T-shirt silhouette
- a sticker
- a cap/bob patch
- a small app icon
- a reaction face
- a medal illustration
- a hidden background cameo

## The Juice

Billy is searching for **The Juice**.

Do not define it.

The Juice can suggest:

- happiness
- purpose
- belonging
- meaning
- stability
- peace
- energy
- success
- aliveness

But the product should never explain it directly.

Rule:

```text
The moment you explain The Juice, The Juice evaporates.
```

Billy repeatedly thinks:

```text
Maybe this is where The Juice is.
```

The comic repeatedly reveals:

```text
Every place gives something.
Every place takes something.
```

## Colourmap Device

Billy carries Colourmap on his phone.

In the comic world, Colourmap is:

- an emotional map
- an inner compass
- a world map
- a weather report for the psyche
- a strange old pirate map translated into an app

It is not GPS. It is not always right.

Core rule:

```text
Maps help. Maps are not reality.
```

Example Colourmap readout:

```text
Current Region:
Babylon

Mood Weather:
Partly excited with incoming uncertainty

Nearby:
Optimization District
Juice Stand
Suspicious Star Activity
Charlie sighting, unconfirmed
```

The Colourmap should avoid moral labels. No region is "good" or "bad." Each region has gifts and
risks.

Example:

```text
Babylon
Gift: clarity
Risk: forgetting wonder

Ocean of Sadness
Gift: depth
Risk: staying too long

Groovy Jungle
Gift: creativity
Risk: seventeen unfinished ideas
```

## Main Questions Before Building

Before implementation, answer these questions.

### 1. What is the reader doing?

Possible answers:

- discovering themselves
- becoming someone
- exploring worlds
- solving mysteries
- learning how to live

Colourmap's answer:

```text
The reader explores worlds to understand themselves.
```

### 2. Is there a destination?

Infinite systems need gravity. Without direction, infinity becomes drift.

The comic can be infinite while still having an invisible center:

```text
The Observatory
```

The Observatory is the place where the reader can step back and understand their own story. The user
may never "finish" the comic, but they can periodically arrive at clearer overview.

### 3. What are the axes?

The worlds are not only locations. They are psychological directions.

Core axes:

- rational world vs creative world
- order vs chaos
- fear vs curiosity
- past vs future
- sadness vs sunlight
- isolation vs connection
- body vs mind
- discipline vs softness
- stability vs adventure

Every choice shifts the map.

### 4. Who remembers?

The system should remember choices, regions visited, repeated attractions, avoided doors, and symbols
that return years later. Memory should create mythological echoes.

Example:

```text
The tree you passed in 2026 returns in 2031 as a door.
```

### 5. Who writes?

AI can generate paths, but the universe needs laws. Without laws, infinite generation becomes soup.

Universe laws:

- every chapter reveals something about being human
- no pure randomness
- humor, beauty, and reflection stay together
- worlds correspond to inner states
- symbols return over time
- text and images remain separate layers
- the user can choose, but the world keeps coherence

## World Structure

The comic is built from expanding regions.

Each region has:

- surface route
- subregions
- hidden rooms
- underground zones
- future expansions
- gifts
- risks
- recurring symbols
- possible Colourmap readouts

Infinite Juice Law:

```text
Every answer reveals deeper layers.
Every region contains surface, subregions, hidden regions, secret zones, and future expansions.
Never close worlds.
```

## Pineapple Planet

Billy begins on a round pineapple-shaped planet.

The green crown at the top becomes expandable continents. At the center is **Juice Square**:

- Billy's apartment
- sofa
- local Juice stand
- small home routes
- normal life that never stays normal

Billy wants ordinary stability. The universe keeps opening doors.

Pineapple Planet is the title world and the symbolic home base of the whole infinite comic.

It should be funny at first glance and profound underneath. A pineapple planet sounds absurd, but it
can carry a universal structure:

```text
sweetness protected by armor
golden life under a rough skin
a crown that is also a wilderness
a fruit that looks like a kingdom
a home that is already a map
```

This makes Pineapple Planet an archetype of the modern self.

The user lives in an age of contradiction:

- everyone wants sweetness, but learns to grow armor
- everyone wants connection, but lives behind interfaces
- everyone wants purpose, but is surrounded by options
- everyone wants rest, but is trained to optimize
- everyone wants freedom, but depends on systems
- everyone wants The Juice, but nobody agrees what it is

Pineapple Planet should reflect this condition without turning into an essay. Its geography is a
symbolic map of our day and age:

```text
Juice Square = desire, commerce, meaning, attention, and public life
Billy's sofa = the longing for rest and ordinary peace
the crown continents = imagination, growth, social worlds, and future expansion
the rough outer shell = defenses, identity, status, and protection
the golden inner land = tenderness, energy, pleasure, and aliveness
the seed tunnels = memory, ancestry, inherited patterns, and subconscious routes
```

Core philosophical question:

```text
What does it mean to search for sweetness in an age that keeps selling substitutes?
```

Pineapple Planet should let the comic talk about modern life indirectly:

- consumer desire without saying "consumerism" too bluntly
- productivity pressure without becoming a work lecture
- attention addiction through Galaxy Gummies and suspicious stars
- spiritual hunger through the Ancestral Pyramid
- system beauty and system capture through Babylon
- creative overflow through Groovy Jungle
- sadness and depth through the Ocean of Sadness
- moral corruption through Crocodile Jack

The planet is not a joke world. It is a mythic diagram disguised as a joke.

Archetypal structure:

```text
Home -> Call -> Wandering -> Temptation -> Descent -> Mirror -> Return -> Wider Home
```

Billy's journey should keep returning to Pineapple Planet changed. The home world must evolve as the
reader evolves. New doors appear. Old locations gain meaning. The same sofa can feel like comfort in
one chapter, avoidance in another, and sacred rest in a later one.

Pineapple Planet is therefore not only where Billy starts. It is the question he keeps returning to:

```text
Can ordinary life become wide enough to hold everything Billy has seen?
```

## Core Regions

### Babylon

Babylon is a hopeful solarpunk city, not a grey dystopia.

It has:

- sunlight
- gardens
- green rooftops
- elegant architecture
- flying transit
- human-scale technology
- clean systems
- smart infrastructure
- people cooperating

Many things in Babylon are genuinely good.

Gift:

```text
clarity, purpose, systems, progress
```

Risk:

```text
slowly becoming trapped inside systems
```

Subregions:

- Optimization District
- Corporate Sector
- Rachel Department
- Forecast Bureau
- Future Planning Catacombs
- Forbidden Basement Disco

Recurring scene:

```text
Billy sits alone on a train in Babylon.
The city is beautiful outside.
He opens Colourmap.
Current state: Searching...
Billy says: Same.
```

Rachel requests reports. Nobody knows who Rachel is.

### Groovy Jungle

Groovy Jungle is music weather, idea plants, creative villages, and unfinished project vines.

Gift:

```text
freedom, creativity, wonder
```

Risk:

```text
too many unfinished ideas
```

Subregions:

- Idea Village
- Burnout Swamp
- Ancient Song Grotto
- Project Valley
- Ancestral Pyramid

### Ancestral Pyramid

Inside Groovy Jungle there is a huge spiritual ancestral pyramid. It should feel ancient, funky,
musical, and sacred without becoming heavy or academic.

Visual language:

- warm jungle night and golden torchlight
- carved fruit symbols
- drums, vines, stars, and old stone
- triangle pineapple prophets
- geometric ancestors
- rhythm patterns on the walls
- a sense that music, memory, and wisdom are older than Billy understands

The prophets are **triangle pineapple prophets**: triangular pineapple beings with leafy crowns,
small expressive faces, and ancient rhythmic authority. They are not literal religious prophets and
should not copy specific religious figures. They are symbolic ancestral teachers: pineapple geometry
with personality, rhythm, and strange wisdom.

The Ancestral Pyramid teaches:

```text
Every generation leaves rhythm.
Every rhythm carries a warning.
Every warning can become music if you listen without trying to own it.
```

The place should include **Funky Monkeys**: chaotic, funny jungle characters who play percussion,
steal snacks, interrupt ceremonies, and sometimes accidentally reveal truth. They are comic relief
and trickster energy. They should never become pure nonsense; their chaos should often expose what
the serious characters are avoiding.

Funky Monkey function:

- break tension
- pull Billy out of over-seriousness
- show group rhythm
- make sacred places feel alive, not museum-like
- create mistakes that open hidden doors

### Crocodile Jack

Crocodile Jack is one of the central recurring characters and the closest thing the universe has to a
main bad guy.

He always looks friendly.

He smiles warmly. He offers deals. He says useful things. He appears helpful, reasonable, and almost
charming. But he repeatedly sabotages worlds from the inside.

Symbolic meaning:

- greed
- craving
- desire without wisdom
- extraction
- false friendliness
- turning sacred things into property
- turning systems into traps
- using people's hopes against them

Important rule:

```text
Crocodile Jack is bad, but not shallow.
```

He should be the worst force in the comic, but still work as a symbol rather than a simple mustache
villain. His danger is that he often sounds practical. He does not destroy worlds by screaming. He
destroys them by making small compromises feel intelligent.

Recurring Jack pattern:

```text
Billy finds a world with a gift.
Crocodile Jack appears near the center of that gift.
He offers to make the gift faster, bigger, easier, or more profitable.
The gift starts turning into a trap.
Billy slowly realizes the Juice has been replaced by extraction.
```

Crocodile Jack appears:

- at the heart of Babylon
- at the heart of the Ancestral Pyramid
- inside Juice markets
- near suspicious contracts
- behind friendly optimization systems
- beside sacred objects that have started to become products

In Babylon, Jack turns clarity into control.

In the Ancestral Pyramid, Jack tries to turn ancestral wisdom into a commodity.

In Groovy Jungle, Jack turns creativity into endless consumption.

In the Mountains of Discipline, Jack turns practice into domination.

In the Ocean of Sadness, Jack turns depth into self-pity that never moves.

Billy should often trust Jack too early because Jack looks like someone who knows where The Juice is.

Jack's recurring line can be:

```text
Relax. I can make this easier.
```

The story should show that not every friendly guide is safe, and not every efficient shortcut is
wisdom.

### Mountains of Happiness

Warm air, festivals, sunlight, and joy.

Gift:

```text
happiness and connection
```

Risk:

```text
forced positivity and fear of sadness
```

### Ocean of Sadness

Beautiful, slow, rainy, with whales, quiet horizons, rays of light, and music carried across the
water.

Gift:

```text
depth, reflection, tenderness, and beauty inside sorrow
```

Risk:

```text
staying too long
```

The Ocean of Sadness should never be only dark. It should contain sources of hope:

- rays of sunlight breaking through heavy clouds
- small boats with lanterns
- whale songs
- people playing blues on floating docks
- old musicians turning sorrow into rhythm
- reflections that become stars on the water
- quiet islands where Billy can rest without pretending to be happy

The blues are important. They show that sadness can become beauty without being denied.

Core lesson:

```text
Sadness is not the opposite of life.
Sometimes sadness is life becoming deep enough to sing.
```

The Ocean teaches Billy that pain can be witnessed, shaped, and shared. It is dangerous only when he
confuses depth with a permanent home.

Possible subregions:

- Blue Lantern Dock
- Whale Memory Channel
- Rain Piano Reef
- Island of Honest Tears
- Hope Ray Horizon

Billy can meet characters who do not try to cheer him up. They play music, sit beside him, and show
that beauty can live inside grief.

### Desert of Loneliness

The Desert of Loneliness is a wide silent region where distances feel too large and every sound
arrives late.

Visual language:

- warm empty dunes
- long shadows
- phone signals that turn into mirages
- abandoned picnic blankets
- distant windows glowing at night
- one small road of footprints
- stars that look close but cannot be touched

Gift:

```text
self-contact, honesty, longing, and the discovery of real connection
```

Risk:

```text
believing separation is the truth of the world
```

The Desert of Loneliness is not only about being alone. It is about the modern condition of being
surrounded by signals while still feeling unseen.

Themes:

- social media without contact
- being in a crowd but not belonging
- waiting for messages
- the fear of reaching out
- the difference between solitude and abandonment
- learning to become good company for yourself without giving up on others

Core lesson:

```text
Loneliness is not proof that nobody exists.
It is the pain of connection being blocked.
```

What Billy learns there:

- solitude can restore him when it is chosen
- isolation shrinks him when it becomes identity
- one honest signal matters more than a thousand weak signals
- asking for company is not failure
- connection often begins with one small visible gesture

Possible subregions:

- Mirage Feed
- Unsent Message Canyon
- The Long Table With One Chair
- Signal Oasis
- Campfire of Strangers
- Valley of Echoes

The Desert should include hope routes:

```text
follow one real voice
light a small campfire
send one true message
walk toward the distant music
sit alone without becoming stone
```

The Desert of Loneliness connects naturally to the Ocean of Sadness, Babylon, and Groovy Jungle. A
lonely Billy may go to Babylon for structure, Groovy Jungle for stimulation, or the Ocean for depth.
The lesson is to discover which route is connection and which route is avoidance.

## Recurring Arcs

### Galaxy Gummies

Billy becomes attached to Galaxy Gummies, tiny star candies that create novelty highs.

Billy says:

```text
I'm not addicted. I just think better with stars.
```

Deeper meaning:

- novelty seeking
- dopamine loops
- distraction
- chasing constant expansion
- confusing sparkle with nourishment

### Shadow Pineapples

Shadow Pineapples are possible Billy versions.

They are not evil. They are paths Billy almost became.

They should often appear silently before being explained.

### Charlie

Hide Charlie in pages, like a visual secret.

Charlie can appear in:

- clouds
- windows
- background paintings
- mountain shapes
- crowds
- reflections

No explanations at first. Let readers speculate.

## Interaction Model

The phone experience should support:

- vertical scroll
- tap to reveal text
- occasional choices
- long silent visual stretches
- map view
- region history
- saved symbols
- left/right/up/down choice moments
- future AI-generated branches inside a coherent universe bible

Reflection is optional. It can be fun, but the core loop should not require the user to journal. The
comic should work when the user only wants to look, laugh, choose, and keep moving.

Default pacing:

```text
3 to 6 visual pages
-> one choice, poll, or funny interruption
-> continue
```

Sometimes a choice can happen very quickly if the context is funny.

Example:

```text
Billy sees a suspicious vending machine labeled by implication, not readable image text.

Choice:
Trust the machine
Kick the machine gently
Ask Colourmap why it is humming
Pretend this is normal
```

Example choices:

```text
Go toward certainty
Enter shadow tunnel
Follow memory river
Stay on sofa
Follow suspicious star
Ignore suspicious star
Go to Babylon
Go to Groovy Jungle
Ask Colourmap
Do not ask Colourmap
```

Choices should not be constant. The user needs time to look, breathe, and feel.

## Social Polls And Collective Answers

The infinite comic should include light social questions inspired by online quiz/poll systems where
the user answers, then sees how other people answered. This makes the comic funny, replayable, and
social without turning it into a feed.

Pattern:

```text
comic scene
-> question
-> 3 to 5 funny answer pills
-> optional write-in
-> aggregate result graph
-> continue story
```

The graph should appear only after the user answers, so the user is not biased by the crowd first.

Answer graphs can show:

- percentage bars
- little fruit icons
- region-specific answer maps
- friend comparison if social features exist
- "people who visited this region also chose..."

Example question:

```text
Do you think Square Billy is obscure?

Answers:
- Not so obscure
- Moderately obscure
- Deeply obscure
- Obscure, but with good shoes
- I am Square Billy
```

Example question:

```text
Is the Friendly Tomato a good guy?

Answers:
- Absolutely, look at that smile
- Suspiciously friendly
- Good guy, terrible timing
- Secretly working for Crocodile Jack
- I refuse to judge tomatoes
```

Example question:

```text
Should Billy follow the humming stairs?

Answers:
- Obviously yes
- No, but he will
- Ask Colourmap
- Eat one Galaxy Gummy first
- Stairs are social constructs
```

The answers should be funny but also reveal something about the reader's style: trust, suspicion,
curiosity, caution, chaos, seriousness, play.

Optional write-in:

```text
Write your own answer
```

Write-ins should be moderated before public display. V1 can keep write-ins private or only use them
as personal notes until moderation exists.

## Progress, Rewards, And Medals

"What does progress mean?" should be one of the great questions of the comic book itself.

Progress must not mean winning life. Billy is not becoming superior. Billy is becoming wider.

Possible progress meanings:

- regions visited
- symbols found
- versions of Billy met
- questions answered
- choices remembered
- medals earned
- friend routes compared
- new layers unlocked on Pineapple Planet
- deeper access to The Observatory

Rewards should feel like travel souvenirs, not productivity badges.

Medal examples:

```text
Ocean Listener
Desert Campfire Keeper
Groovy Jungle Drummer
Babylon Intern, Unpaid But Glowing
Galaxy Gummy Survivor
Square Billy Witness
Friendly Tomato Investigator
Crocodile Jack Suspicion Level 1
Triangle Pineapple Prophet Apprentice
```

The user can see:

- which regions they visited
- which medals they earned
- which friends travelled to the same regions
- which friends answered the same question differently
- which Billy versions they encountered

This creates social texture without requiring public performance.

Friend comparison should stay gentle:

```text
3 friends visited Ocean of Sadness.
1 stayed for the blues.
2 followed the ray of light.
```

Avoid leaderboards. The comic is not a competition.

## Image And Text Rules

Images contain no readable text.

Text overlays are separate app-rendered layers so the comic can adapt, translate, and evolve.

This separation also allows image reuse. Some images can become loops, recurring scenes, or reusable
visual plates while the text changes depending on context.

Example:

```text
same image: Billy sitting on the Babylon train
version A text: first arrival, wonder
version B text: later return, exhaustion
version C text: after Ocean of Sadness, tenderness
version D text: after meeting Crocodile Jack, suspicion
```

The image is the stage. The text is the living layer.

Long term, text can adapt to:

- choices the user made
- regions already visited
- medals earned
- Billy versions already encountered
- previous answers to poll questions
- optional reflections the user wrote
- repeated symbols in the user's journey
- whether the user is in relaxation mode or adventure mode

The comic should support reusable visual loops:

- train rides
- sofa moments
- walking roads
- ocean docks
- desert campfires
- Groovy Jungle music circles
- Babylon office scenes
- Colourmap phone readouts
- Crocodile Jack appearing too friendly

These loops make the world feel familiar and let the story become more personal without needing a
new generated image for every small variation.

Future adaptive text principle:

```text
Images can repeat.
Meaning should evolve.
```

This can become one of the most fun parts of the system. The reader may recognize an image from
earlier, but the text now knows what they have lived through with Billy.

## Symbolic Scene Library

The comic should build a large library of highly symbolic reusable images. These are archetypal
scenes that can carry different meanings depending on the route, text, music, region, and previous
choices.

The images should be simple enough to read instantly, but deep enough to return many times.

Core symbolic scenes:

- Billy climbing a mountain
- Billy crossing a bridge
- Billy meeting a stranger in a hood
- Billy standing before a door
- Billy walking along a river
- Billy on a train
- Billy at a campfire
- Billy looking into a mirror
- Billy entering a cave
- Billy watching a sunrise
- Billy lost in fog
- Billy carrying a heavy backpack
- Billy finding a small light
- Billy at a fork in the road
- Billy in a crowd but feeling alone
- Billy sitting on a roof above Babylon
- Billy hearing music from behind trees
- Billy watching stars fall into a jar
- Billy standing before the Ancestral Pyramid
- Billy facing Crocodile Jack's friendly smile

These are not one-use illustrations. They are visual instruments.

Example: hooded stranger.

The same base image can become:

```text
in Babylon: a recruiter from the Forecast Bureau
in Groovy Jungle: a drummer who knows a hidden rhythm
in Ocean of Sadness: someone who sits silently beside Billy
in Desert of Loneliness: a mirage or a real friend
in Mirror Valley: a future Billy
near Crocodile Jack: a trap disguised as guidance
```

Example: bridge.

```text
first use: simple crossing
later use: choosing connection
after Ocean of Sadness: returning from grief
after Babylon: escaping over-control
after Groovy Jungle: finishing one idea instead of starting twenty
```

The symbolic scene library should support:

- reusable image assets
- alternate text overlays
- different choices on the same image
- region-specific recolors or small variants
- hidden characters such as Charlie
- poll questions attached to the scene
- future AI-generated meaning while preserving the original visual plate

This lets the comic become deep without needing every page to be visually overloaded. One strong
symbol per page is often better than ten ideas in the same image.

Visual rule:

```text
Symbolic, not cluttered.
One main archetypal action per image.
Enough empty space for text and later choices.
```

Recommended rhythm:

```text
visual page
tap
small line of narration
tap
choice or next visual page
```

Sometimes:

```text
question
2 to 5 visual pages
silent symbolic fragments
choice
```

## Product Placement

Billy belongs in **Entertainment**, not Education.

Entertainment can eventually hold playful, reflective experiences that are valuable because they
restore imagination and widen the user, not because they teach a formal lesson.

Possible placement:

```text
Entertainment
  Infinite Comic
  Billy Pineapple
  Relaxation journeys
  Interactive myth worlds
```

Education can link to Billy only as a related playful world, but the primary home should remain
Entertainment.

## Education Echoes

Billy is not an Education program, but it should echo Colourmap Education. The comic should be fun,
absurd, and relaxing while still carrying real learning and depth.

Rule:

```text
Entertainment first.
Education underneath.
```

The user should not feel like they opened a lesson. But the worlds, choices, and characters can
quietly reflect the same principles as the education programs:

- Jung: shadow selves, symbols, dreams, Mirror Valley, Billy versions
- Freire: dialogue, power, collective agency, Babylon systems, people learning to name reality
- Thich Nhat Hanh: presence, breathing, peace, Ocean of Sadness, quiet walking paths
- Gandhi: small truthful acts, inner freedom, nonviolence, discipline without hatred
- Clear and Allen: habits, capture, organization, trusted systems, Babylon office loops
- Personality Map: order/chaos, party/peace, rest/forcing, anger/boundary, subconscious tension
- Process Maps: pressure -> regulation -> guilt -> transformation -> return

The comic should translate these ideas into story instead of explaining them directly.

Examples:

```text
Jung becomes Shadow Pineapples and Mirror Valley.
Freire becomes a village learning to name Crocodile Jack's contracts.
Thich Nhat Hanh becomes Billy sitting by the Ocean without trying to fix it.
Gandhi becomes one tiny truthful action that changes a crowd.
Clear and Allen become Billy learning that a notebook can hold the chaos better than his head.
```

The goal is not stupid crazy randomness. The comic can be crazy, but the craziness should have a
spine. Each absurd world should carry one real human question.

Quality rule:

```text
Every funny region needs a hidden serious question.
Every serious region needs enough play to stay alive.
```

## Relaxation Mode

This is especially important as a "when you need to relax" experience.

Relaxation mode should:

- reduce decisions
- slow pacing
- use longer silent visual stretches
- offer soft choices only
- avoid achievement pressure
- let the user wander without goals
- occasionally ask one reflective question

Example soft choices:

```text
Sit by the window
Walk toward the warm lights
Listen to the strange music
Ask the map what it sees
Close the map and keep walking
```

## AI Generation Constraints

AI can create expansions, but only inside the universe bible.

Required inputs for generated branches:

- current region
- user's previous choices
- active psychological axis
- Billy's current mood weather
- recurring symbols already encountered
- desired pacing: funny, quiet, strange, reflective, relaxing, intense

AI must return structured data:

```ts
type InfiniteComicNode = {
  id: string;
  regionId: string;
  title: string;
  imagePrompt: string;
  narration: string[];
  choices: InfiniteComicChoice[];
  symbols: string[];
  gift?: string;
  risk?: string;
  moodWeather?: string;
};

type InfiniteComicChoice = {
  id: string;
  label: string;
  axisShift: string;
  targetNodeId?: string;
};
```

The app owns state, memory, safety, and coherence. AI proposes scenes; the app validates and stores
them.

## Safety And Tone

No final answers. No heaven/hell structure. No villains as simple evil.

Every system contains truth and limitation.

The comic should never say a user's choice proves something fixed about them. It should say:

```text
This path reveals one way you are moving today.
```

## V1 Scope

Do not build infinity first.

The first 100-page production arc is specified in [Billy Pineapple First 100 Pages](billy-first-100-pages.md).

V1 should prove the loop with one small playable branch:

```text
Pineapple Planet
-> Babylon
-> Groovy Jungle
-> Ocean of Sadness
-> The Observatory
```

V1 features:

- Billy protagonist
- vertical comic reader
- text overlays separate from images
- Colourmap device readout
- three regions
- one soft choice point per region
- one hidden Charlie appearance
- one saved journey summary
- one relaxation pacing mode

V1 non-goals:

- fully dynamic AI world generation
- multiplayer
- public sharing
- complex inventory
- combat
- scoring
- social feeds
- infinite generated image pipeline

## Done When

- The user can enter the infinite comic from an Entertainment or relaxation surface.
- The user can read a short Billy story on phone without friction.
- Images and text are separate layers.
- The user can make at least three meaningful choices.
- Colourmap appears as a map/weather device inside the story.
- Each region shows gift and risk without moral judgment.
- The session can end at The Observatory with a simple reflection.
- The experience feels relaxing even if the subject becomes deep.
